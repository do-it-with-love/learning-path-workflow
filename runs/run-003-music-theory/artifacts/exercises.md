# Exercises — Music Theory for Score Reading and Analysis

## Summary

The piano is the answer key throughout this practice set: almost every exercise pairs
"write/predict it" with "play it and listen," because for a pianist at this level
hearing a chord, interval, or cadence confirms or corrects a written answer far more
reliably than re-reading a rule. Retrieval leads every exercise — flashcard-style
recall, cold sight-reading, labelling before checking — never passive re-reading.
Bass-clef sight-reading fluency, the running weakness named in the brief, gets a short
(around 10-minute) timed drill in every module rather than one dedicated module,
carrying a single measured benchmark (measures-per-minute, first logged in Module 1)
that the learner can watch improve across the path. Harmonic-analysis skill is built
the same way: every module from Module 4 onward includes a synthesis exercise that
extends the analysis of the capstone piece's opening bars a little further, so the
Module 10 capstone document is an assembly and completion job, not a cold start.
Practice totals roughly **12.7 core hours** (about 13 hours if every optional stretch
is taken), against the curriculum's 40-hour module-content envelope — averaging a touch
over an hour a week of the 4-hour weekly budget, leaving the remaining time for the
reading resources the learner explicitly preferred. Individual module practice loads
are sized to the module's stated hours (45 minutes for the short Module 7 on cadences,
up to about 2 hours for the heavier Module 4 and the Module 10 capstone), on the
assumption that resource-reading time fills the rest of each module's allocation.

## Findings

### Module 1: Key Signatures, the Circle of Fifths & Reading Fluency

**1. Drill — key-signature flashcards**
Task: Make (or use) flashcards for all major key signatures up to 4 sharps/flats. Draw
20 at random, name the key aloud in under 3 seconds each, then play the tonic triad on
piano to confirm. Repeat the full set across the first three practice sessions.
Success criteria: 18/20 correct with under-3-second recall on the third pass.
Time: 15 min.
When: start of each of the first three sessions in this module.
If stuck: drop to signatures with 2 or fewer sharps/flats, re-read the circle-of-fifths
explanation, then add one sharp/flat side at a time.

**2. Fluency drill — bass-clef timed reading (baseline)**
Task: Pick a short, unfamiliar piece or excerpt and sight-read its bass-clef line alone
for 1 minute at a steady tempo. Count how many measures you complete without stopping
and log the number — this is your fluency baseline. Repeat with a fresh excerpt in each
session this module.
Success criteria: your measures-per-minute count increases from session 1 to session 3,
with no more than one stop per line by the third attempt.
Time: 10 min.
When: first thing, every session of this module.
If stuck: halve the tempo with a metronome, then raise it 5 bpm per successful pass.

**3. Application — naming keys from unfamiliar signatures**
Task: Take 4 short excerpts you haven't seen before, in 4 different key signatures
(0-4 sharps or flats). Without playing, name each key from the signature alone, then
play the opening two bars to check whether the melody's implied tonic agrees. Note in
one sentence why any excerpt felt ambiguous (e.g., it could sound minor).
Success criteria: correct key named, before playing, for at least 3 of 4; for any miss,
you can state in one sentence why it was ambiguous.
Time: 20 min.
When: mid-module session.
If stuck: write out the order of sharps/flats for that signature on paper before naming
the key.

**4. Synthesis — previewing the capstone's key**
Task: Look only at the key signature of the capstone piece's opening two bars. Name its
key and predict which accidentals you'd expect to see later. Then play those two bars
slowly, hands together, and log your reading speed against Exercise 2's benchmark.
Success criteria: correct key stated with your reasoning; a logged reading-speed number
you can compare against later modules.
Time: 15 min.
When: last session of this module.
If stuck: work the treble line alone first, then check your answer against this
module's key-signature list.

### Module 2: Intervals — Number and Quality

**1. Drill — interval flashcards, played not just named**
Task: Using note-pair flashcards spanning both clefs, identify the number and quality
of 20 intervals, then play each one on the piano to confirm — does a major 3rd really
sound brighter than a minor 3rd to you?
Success criteria: 16/20 correct on first pass; every miss corrected and understood
after playing it.
Time: 15 min.
When: start of the first two sessions.
If stuck: limit the set to 2nds, 3rds, and octaves until consistently accurate, then
add larger intervals.

**2. Fluency drill — bass-clef reading with interval spotting**
Task: Sight-read a bass-clef line for 90 seconds. Every time you cross a leap bigger
than a 3rd, call out the interval before continuing.
Success criteria: fewer than 2 reading stops, and at least 4 correctly named intervals
during the pass.
Time: 10 min.
When: every session.
If stuck: read for speed only first, then reintroduce interval-naming once the reading
itself is smooth.

**3. Application — leaps from real music**
Task: Pick 6 melodic leaps from a piece you're learning or sight-reading, in either
clef. Name each interval on paper, then play it and check whether your ear agrees
before looking at your written answer. For any mismatch, diagnose whether the error was
in note-naming or in quality.
Success criteria: 5 of 6 correctly identified before playing; a one-sentence diagnosis
written for the miss.
Time: 20 min.
When: mid-module.
If stuck: restrict to leaps of a 4th or smaller.

**4. Synthesis — intervals in the capstone opening**
Task: Label the interval between every pair of consecutive melody notes in both hands
for the capstone piece's first two bars, then play those bars to confirm the labels
sound right. Check whether any scale-like run in the passage matches the major-scale
step pattern (W-W-H-W-W-W-H).
Success criteria: all intervals correctly labelled and confirmed by ear; a clear
statement of whether the passage follows the plain major-scale pattern or departs
from it.
Time: 20 min.
When: final session of this module.
If stuck: work one hand's line at a time.

### Module 3: Minor Scales & Relative/Parallel Keys

**1. Drill — write then play three minor forms**
Task: For 5 major keys already practised in Module 1, write the natural, harmonic, and
melodic minor scale on the relative minor tonic, then immediately play each ascending
and descending, listening for where the raised leading tone (harmonic) and raised
6th/7th (melodic ascending) change the sound.
Success criteria: for at least 4 of 5 keys, all three forms play without a stumble and
you can name which scale degree changed between forms.
Time: 20 min.
When: first two sessions.
If stuck: work A minor alone (no signature to manage) until the three forms are secure,
then add sharps/flats.

**2. Fluency drill — spotting the raised leading tone**
Task: Sight-read a short bass-clef passage in a minor key for 90 seconds, watching for
the accidental that doesn't appear in the key signature.
Success criteria: at least 3 such accidentals correctly anticipated and played without
backtracking.
Time: 10 min.
When: every session.
If stuck: pencil-mark the accidentals first, play through once with the marks visible,
then remove them and repeat.

**3. Application — major or relative minor?**
Task: Take 3 unfamiliar excerpts in a minor key. From the signature alone, name the two
candidate keys (major and relative minor), then play the final cadence to decide which
it actually is, writing one sentence of evidence (leading tone present, final chord
quality, etc.).
Success criteria: correct decision on at least 2 of 3, each with written evidence you
could check without replaying it.
Time: 20 min.
When: mid-module.
If stuck: check only "is scale degree 7 raised anywhere?" and ignore other cues first.

**4. Synthesis — revisiting Module 1's excerpts**
Task: Return to the 4 excerpts from Module 1's key-naming exercise. For any you now
suspect are minor rather than major (or vice versa), redo the call using this module's
leading-tone/cadence method and note whether your earlier answer changes.
Success criteria: a stated major-or-relative-minor call, with evidence, for all 4
excerpts.
Time: 15 min.
When: final session.
If stuck: re-read your Module 1 notes on those excerpts before redoing the call.

### Module 4: Triad Construction & Quality

**1. Drill — build and play all four triad qualities**
Task: From 12 random roots split across 3 sessions (4 roots per session), write then
play a major, minor, diminished, and augmented triad on each root, playing all four
back to back on each root to hear the quality change.
Success criteria: at least 14 of 16 triads correct per session before playing; by ear,
you can identify which quality you just played without looking at the page.
Time: 20 min per session (3 sessions).
When: sessions 1, 2, and 3 of this module.
If stuck: drop augmented triads first and master major/minor/diminished, adding
augmented once those are solid.

**2. Fluency drill — naming triad quality while reading**
Task: Sight-read a bass-clef passage built from block or broken triads for 90 seconds,
naming the quality of each chord as you play it.
Success criteria: at least 4 triad qualities correctly identified during the pass, with
fewer than 2 stops.
Time: 10 min.
When: every session.
If stuck: slow the tempo and isolate each chord before moving to the next.

**3. Application — root, quality, and inversion by ear first**
Task: From a piece with block or broken chords in the left hand, pick 6 chords.
Decide root, quality, and whether it's root position or an inversion by ear first (does
the bass note sound like the true root, or does the chord feel "tipped over"?), then
check on paper.
Success criteria: correct quality on 5 of 6; a written explanation of your inversion
reasoning for any miss.
Time: 20 min.
When: mid-to-late module.
If stuck: restrict to root-position chords until quality identification is reliable,
then reintroduce inversions.

**4. Synthesis — triads in the capstone's opening**
Task: In the capstone piece's opening 4 bars, find every left-hand or implied triad and
label root, quality, and position. Play just those 4 bars slowly, isolating each chord
to confirm the labels.
Success criteria: every triad in the 4 bars labelled and confirmed by playing; a clear
statement of which, if any, are inverted.
Time: 25 min.
When: final session.
If stuck: work the first 2 bars only, or hands separately.

### Module 5: Primary Triads in a Key

**1. Drill — spell I, IV, V, vi from memory**
Task: For 6 major keys, write then play the I, IV, V and vi triads from memory, without
writing out the scale first.
Success criteria: at least 20 of 24 triads correct without the scaffold; for misses,
note whether the root or the quality was wrong.
Time: 15 min.
When: first session.
If stuck: allow yourself to write scale degrees 1, 4, 5, 6 first as a scaffold, removing
it once you reach 80% accuracy.

**2. Fluency drill — naming I/IV/V while reading**
Task: Sight-read a bass-clef line built on I-IV-V-I progressions for 90 seconds, calling
out "I," "IV," or "V" as you play each chord.
Success criteria: at least 5 chords correctly labelled, no more than 1 stop.
Time: 10 min.
When: every session.
If stuck: pencil-label the chords, play through once with labels visible, cover them,
and repeat.

**3. Application — locating primary triads by feel**
Task: In a piece you know or a short new excerpt, mark every place the left hand plays
what feels like a tonic, subdominant, or dominant chord — by ear first — then check each
guess against the I/IV/V/vi set you spelled in Exercise 1.
Success criteria: at least 4 primary-triad instances located, with your "feeling"
judgment matching the spelled-triad check for at least 3 of them.
Time: 20 min.
When: mid-module.
If stuck: work with a piece entirely in C major first, where I/IV/V are all white-key
triads.

**4. Synthesis — matching Module 4's triads to I/IV/V/vi**
Task: Return to the capstone's opening 4 bars (already labelled for root/quality/
position in Module 4) and match each triad to the key's I, IV, V, vi — or "none of
these" — using the primary-triad set you spelled this module.
Success criteria: every triad in the 4 bars matched consistently with Module 4's labels.
Time: 15 min.
When: final session.
If stuck: re-spell just the I, IV, V, vi triads for the capstone's key before comparing.

### Module 6: Roman-Numeral & Lead-Sheet Analysis

**1. Drill — the full diatonic triad set**
Task: For 3 major keys, write the full diatonic triad set (I through vii°) with correct
upper/lower-case Roman numerals and quality symbols, then play each triad up the scale,
listening for the major-major-minor-major-major-minor-diminished pattern.
Success criteria: all 7 Roman numerals correctly cased for at least 2 of 3 keys; the
full set plays without stopping to recalculate quality.
Time: 20 min.
When: first two sessions.
If stuck: use only I, ii, IV, V, vi (the objective's required set) and skip iii/vii°
until those five are solid.

**2. Fluency drill — Roman numerals while reading**
Task: Sight-read a bass-clef passage on a I-ii-V-I-style progression for 90 seconds,
saying the Roman numeral (with correct case) aloud as you play each chord.
Success criteria: at least 5 chords correctly labelled with proper case, fewer than 2
stops.
Time: 10 min.
When: every session.
If stuck: revert to Module 5's I/IV/V naming, then add ii once that's automatic.

**3. Application — two labelling systems, one judgement call**
Task: Label every chord of an unfamiliar 4-8 bar passage with both a Roman numeral and
a lead-sheet letter name. For any chord readable two plausible ways, decide which is
more likely from the surrounding chords and write one sentence justifying the choice.
Success criteria: correct Roman-numeral/lead-sheet pair for at least 80% of chords; a
defensible written justification for any ambiguous one.
Time: 25 min.
When: mid-module.
If stuck: find the lead-sheet letter names first, then derive the Roman numerals from
those.

**4. Synthesis — first Roman-numeral pass on the capstone**
Task: Label every chord of the capstone piece's opening phrase (4-8 bars, both hands)
with Roman numerals, cross-checking each against the primary-triad locations already
found in Modules 4-5. Flag and investigate any disagreement.
Success criteria: a complete, internally consistent Roman-numeral labelling of the
opening phrase, with any unresolved contradiction explicitly noted in writing.
Time: 30 min.
When: final session — this pass is reused directly in Module 10.
If stuck: work one hand or one chord at a time, and re-read this module's material on
diatonic triad quality patterns.

### Module 7: Cadences

**1. Drill — naming cadences by sound**
Task: Play through 8 two-chord cadence pairs (2 each of perfect, plagal, imperfect, and
interrupted), naming the type before playing, then confirming by ear (does it feel
finished, "amen"-like, hanging, or surprising?).
Success criteria: at least 6 of 8 correctly identified by ear, even where the pre-play
guess was wrong.
Time: 15 min.
When: first session.
If stuck: distinguish perfect vs. imperfect only before adding plagal and interrupted.

**2. Fluency drill — cadence-spotting at speed**
Task: Sight-read the last 2 bars of 4 unfamiliar short pieces (phrase endings only) in
both clefs, naming the cadence type as fast as you can read it.
Success criteria: correct cadence type named for at least 3 of 4 with minimal
hesitation on the notes themselves.
Time: 10 min.
When: every session.
If stuck: play the final two chords slowly, hands separately, before naming.

**3. Application — cadence as key evidence**
Task: Return to 2 excerpts from Module 3's key-identification exercise. Name the
cadence type ending each, and decide whether it confirms or corrects your earlier key
call, writing one sentence either way.
Success criteria: correct cadence naming for both, with a clear written confirm/correct
statement.
Time: 10 min.
When: mid-module.
If stuck: re-read Module 3's leading-tone check before naming the cadence.

**4. Synthesis — confirming the capstone's key**
Task: Find the clearest cadence in the capstone's opening phrase (likely at its end),
name its type, and use it to double-check the key assigned in earlier modules, writing
one sentence confirming or revising.
Success criteria: cadence correctly identified and explicitly used to confirm or revise
the key call.
Time: 10 min.
When: final session.
If stuck: look at the bar immediately before any double bar line or clear phrase break.

### Module 8: Seventh Chords & Extended Analysis

**1. Drill — spell and resolve V7**
Task: For 6 major keys, write and play the V7 chord (root, 3rd, 5th, 7th), resolving it
to I immediately after, listening for how the 7th pulls toward the 3rd of I.
Success criteria: correctly spelled V7 for at least 5 of 6 keys; the resolution "pull"
described in your own words for at least 3.
Time: 15 min.
When: first session.
If stuck: build V7 as the already-known V triad plus a minor 7th above the root, as an
explicit scaffold.

**2. Fluency drill — spotting V7 while reading**
Task: Sight-read a bass-clef passage containing at least one V7-to-I resolution for
90 seconds, calling "V7" the instant you spot four stacked chord tones on the dominant.
Success criteria: at least 1 V7 correctly spotted and named, no more than 2 stops
overall.
Time: 10 min.
When: every session.
If stuck: pencil-circle the V7 before sight-reading, then remove the mark and repeat.

**3. Application — V7 in context**
Task: In an unfamiliar 4-6 bar passage, find and label a V7 with a Roman numeral, then
decide whether it resolves as expected to I or does something unexpected. Write one
sentence on what you found.
Success criteria: V7 correctly located and labelled, with a reasoned one-sentence
account of its resolution.
Time: 20 min.
When: mid-module.
If stuck: play the bass line alone first to spot the typical V7-to-I leap/step.

**4. Synthesis — upgrading V to V7 in the capstone**
Task: Recheck the Module 6 Roman-numeral pass on the capstone's opening phrase for any
V chord that should actually be relabelled V7, and update the analysis sheet.
Success criteria: the capstone opening-phrase analysis sheet is updated and internally
consistent, with any V7 correctly marked.
Time: 20 min.
When: final session.
If stuck: check each V chord's notes against the four notes of a V7 spelled in
Exercise 1.

### Module 9: Phrase Structure & Musical Form

**1. Drill — marking phrase boundaries by ear**
Task: Play through 3 short pieces (8-16 bars each) you can already sight-read, marking
every phrase boundary by ear (where does the music "breathe" or land?), then check each
against a genuine cadence using Module 7's identification skill.
Success criteria: for at least 2 of 3 pieces, ear-marked boundaries match a genuine
cadence point at least 80% of the time.
Time: 20 min.
When: first session.
If stuck: clap or tap the pulse while listening to feel phrase length (often 4 or 8
bars) before marking boundaries.

**2. Fluency drill — full-piece reading-speed check**
Task: Sight-read a full short piece, both clefs, without stopping, timing yourself
against the measures-per-minute benchmark logged since Module 1.
Success criteria: your current measures-per-minute exceeds your Module 1 baseline.
Time: 10 min.
When: every session.
If stuck: if speed has plateaued, drop tempo 10% and prioritise zero-stop accuracy,
then rebuild speed.

**3. Application — binary or ternary?**
Task: Take 2 unfamiliar short pieces (or two contrasting sections of one). Using
cadence points and whether material repeats or contrasts, decide binary (AB) or ternary
(ABA). For any hard call, write one sentence on what made it ambiguous.
Success criteria: correct call on at least 1 of 2, self-checkable against the piece's
own repeat marks or by listening again; a clear account of your reasoning either way.
Time: 25 min.
When: mid-module.
If stuck: listen for whether the opening idea returns near the end — if so, it's at
least ternary-leaning.

**4. Synthesis — mapping the capstone's opening section**
Task: Using Modules 6-8's chord analysis plus this module's tools, map the capstone's
opening section: mark every phrase boundary, name each cadence, and state whether the
two halves are harmonically similar (both tonic) or contrasting (a move to a new key
area, e.g. the dominant).
Success criteria: a complete phrase map with cadence types and a harmonic-relationship
statement, consistent with your existing chord labels.
Time: 30 min.
When: final session — explicitly prep for Module 10.
If stuck: work in 4-bar chunks, reusing Module 7's cadence-finding method chunk by
chunk.

### Module 10: Capstone — Full Analysis of a Beginner Piece

**1. Drill — final fluency check**
Task: Sight-read the entire capstone piece once through, both hands, at your fastest
stop-free tempo, and log the final measures-per-minute figure next to your Module 1 and
Module 9 numbers.
Success criteria: no more than 2 stops for the whole piece, and a clearly faster figure
than your Module 1 baseline.
Time: 10 min.
When: the very first session of this module, before analysis work begins.
If stuck: split the piece into two halves, time each separately, then add the results.

**2. Application — completing the chord labelling**
Task: Extend the Roman-numeral/lead-sheet labelling from the opening phrase (Module 6)
to the entire movement, section by section. For any chord that doesn't fit the
I-vi-IV-V7-ii vocabulary cleanly, decide your best-supported reading and flag it
explicitly as uncertain rather than guessing silently.
Success criteria: every chord has a label — confident or explicitly flagged uncertain —
and when you play through with labels visible, none of the "confident" labels sound
wrong against what you hear.
Time: 60 min (split across up to 2 sessions).
When: sessions 2-3 of this module.
If stuck: use Modules 6 and 8's worked passages as models for chords of similar shape,
or narrow scope to just the sections not yet covered.

**3. Synthesis — the written capstone document**
Task: Complete the full formal map (phrase boundaries, cadences, binary/ternary call,
harmonic relationship between sections) for the whole piece using Module 9's method.
Then write one connected analysis document (roughly 1-2 pages) stating the key with
evidence, walking through the chord progression in prose using your Roman-numeral
labels, and explaining the form and how the sections relate. Read it aloud once,
checking that every claim can be pointed to on the score.
Success criteria: the document covers key, chords, and form in connected prose (not
just labels); every sentence can be pointed to on the score when read aloud — a
sentence that can't is a guess and should be revised or flagged.
Time: 45 min.
When: final one to two sessions of this module.
If stuck: draft three short paragraphs (key, chords, form) separately first, then
combine them into one flowing document.

**4. Stretch (optional) — a second reading**
Task: For one section of the piece, write a short alternative paragraph analysing it
from an angle not already emphasised in the main document (e.g., if the main analysis
focused on harmony, add a phrase-rhythm observation, or vice versa).
Success criteria: the alternative paragraph makes a new, checkable claim not already
covered in the main document.
Time: 15 min.
When: after the main document is complete, if time and energy remain.
If stuck: skip — this is optional and not required for the capstone deliverable.

## Open Questions

- **Split between resource time and practice time per module is not visible here.**
  This artifact sizes practice to fit comfortably inside each module's stated total
  hours (e.g., 45 minutes of practice within Module 7's 2-hour budget, leaving 1h15m
  for reading), but the actual resource lengths are chosen by the curator running in
  parallel. If a module's resources turn out to run long, `effort-budget-aggregator`
  or `schedule-planner` should flag it rather than silently compressing practice time,
  since the practice — especially the fluency drills and the cumulative capstone
  synthesis chain — is where the retention comes from.
- **Ear-based self-checking has a ceiling this early.** Several success criteria rely on
  "does this sound right to you" (interval quality, triad colour, cadence feel). For a
  beginner-intermediate pianist with no formal ear-training background, this is a
  genuinely useful check but not an infallible one — a few misjudged sounds may go
  uncaught until a later module's written cross-check (e.g., Module 6 catching a
  Module 4 error) surfaces the mistake indirectly. A tutor listening alongside the
  learner in the first few modules would catch these faster than the self-check chain
  does.
- **Judging the written capstone document's prose quality is not fully self-checkable.**
  The "point to it on the score" check in Module 10's synthesis exercise verifies that
  claims are grounded, but it cannot judge whether the analysis is well-organised,
  clearly written, or pitched at the right level of detail for a reader other than the
  learner. A tutor or knowledgeable reader would add real value here that no
  self-check can replace.
- **Ambiguous chord and form readings in real (not constructed) excerpts.** The
  application exercises in Modules 6, 8, and 9 deliberately include passages that admit
  more than one reasonable analysis, by design — but where a passage is genuinely
  contested even among trained ears (e.g., a chord that could plausibly be read two
  ways with no clearly stronger case), the learner's own reasoning is the only check
  available. This is flagged rather than smoothed over: a tutor's judgement is the only
  way to fully resolve a truly ambiguous case.
