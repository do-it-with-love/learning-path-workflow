# Curriculum — Music Theory for Score Reading and Analysis

## Summary

Ten modules, sequenced along the ABRSM-aligned dependency chain the baseline assessment
already established: rudiments (assumed known) → key signatures → intervals → minor
scales → triad construction → primary/diatonic harmony → Roman-numeral analysis →
cadences → seventh chords → phrase and form → capstone analysis. The path starts at
Grade 1 content, not Grade 0, because the baseline confirms note names and the major
scale are solid — re-teaching them would waste hours the learner doesn't have. Two-clef
sight-reading fluency is deliberately **not** a separate module: the baseline shows
literacy (not absence) in both clefs, just below tempo, so a short fluency drill is
folded into the practice time of every module instead of being boxed into one week —
this matches how sight-reading actually improves (little and often) and avoids a
theory-free opening module that would front-load the least rewarding material. The goal
explicitly names a concrete deliverable — "a written analysis of one capstone piece" —
so a capstone module (Module 10) is included, built around a single standard
beginner-appropriate work rather than a generic "review week." Total estimated hours:
40 of the 48-hour budget (4 hrs/week × 12 weeks), leaving 8 hours (~17%) of slack for
revision and life, slightly above the requested 15% floor because Module 4 (triad
construction) and Module 6 (Roman-numeral analysis) are the two highest-risk modules
for running long. Deliberately excluded: modulation to distant keys, four-part
voice-leading, figured bass realisation, secondary dominants, and full sonata-form
analysis — these belong to Grade 6+ theory and are out of scope for a 48-hour beginner
path to single-piece analysis.

Module 9's prerequisite list should read "6, 7" only — the "9" is a typo carried over
from drafting and is corrected here: Module 9 depends on Modules 6 and 7 (harmonic
labelling and cadence identification), not on itself.

Total estimated hours: **40** against a budget of 48 (4 hrs/week × 12 weeks), leaving
8 hours (~17%) of slack.

**Capstone piece.** Recommended: Clementi, *Sonatina in C major*, Op. 36, No. 1, first
movement (Allegro) — one of the "earlier and easier" Clementi sonatas that were
reclassified as sonatinas, confirmed by the Wikipedia article on Clementi as a
standard, widely used beginner teaching piece with freely available scores (IMSLP,
Mutopia). Its harmonic vocabulary (primary triads, simple V7, clear binary/sonata-type
phrase structure) matches exactly what Modules 1–9 teach, with no forward reference.
The specific edition and a confirmed key/bar-count should be checked against a live
score by the resource curator before Module 6 begins, since the Wikipedia biography
confirms the sonatina's existence and pedagogical standing but not movement-level
harmonic detail — that must come from the score itself, not from this artifact.

### Deliberately excluded

A fuller (Grade 6–8 ABRSM-equivalent or undergraduate first-year) treatment would also
include, none of which this path attempts:

- Modulation to closely related or distant keys within a piece, and pivot-chord analysis.
- Four-part chorale-style voice leading and part-writing rules.
- Figured bass realisation.
- Secondary dominants and other chromatic/altered chords beyond a plain V7.
- Full sonata-form analysis (exposition/development/recapitulation) — the capstone uses
  only a short, simple movement, not a full development-section work.
- Species counterpoint.
- Formal aural/dictation training beyond the informal ear-checks embedded in practice.
- Transposition practice beyond what's needed to recognise a key signature.
- Any repertoire beyond solo piano (the learner's stated instrument and modality).

## Open Questions

- **Exact capstone edition/key.** Clementi's Op. 36 No. 1 is named here on the strength
  of the Wikipedia Clementi article (confirms it as one of the standard, widely
  reclassified "easier" sonatinas with free scores at IMSLP/Mutopia) but that article
  does not give movement-level key or bar data. The resource curator should confirm the
  specific key signature and length against a live score before Module 6 practice
  material is finalised; if the movement turns out to need a concept outside Modules
  1–9 (e.g. a secondary dominant), the curriculum will need a one-off retry to either
  swap the piece or add a short bridging note in Module 8.
- **Fluency strand sizing.** The baseline assessment's placement check (Q8) may reveal
  that two-clef fluency is weaker than assessed. Because fluency practice is folded into
  every module's hours rather than isolated, a lower placement-check score should be
  handled by the learner spending relatively more of each module's existing practice
  time on fluency drills, not by adding a new module — there is no slack hour budget
  left to add one (40 of 48 hours are already allocated, leaving only 8).
  **Arithmetic check:** if the placement check score is 0–2 (baseline lower than
  assessed) and the learner genuinely needs a dedicated rudiments-and-fluency module
  before Module 1, the honest fix is not free: it would require either extending the
  horizon beyond 12 weeks or cutting one existing module (most cuttable: Module 8,
  seventh chords, since outcome 3 only requires "simple" sevenths and could be reduced
  to recognition-only within Module 6's hours). This is flagged here rather than
  silently resolved because it cannot be fixed within the stated 48-hour budget if it
  occurs.
- **Module 5 vs. Module 6 boundary.** Whether "primary triads in a key" (Module 5) and
  "Roman-numeral labelling of those same triads" (Module 6) should be one module or two
  was a judgement call. They were split because Module 5 is pure recall/spelling
  (Grade 2–3 content) while Module 6 is the first true analytical/functional skill
  (Grade 4–5), and merging them risked a module that both introduces new vocabulary and
  demands analytical fluency in the same 3–5 hour block — likely to run long for a
  learner still building speed.
- **Module 9 order relative to Module 8.** Phrase/form (Module 9) is placed after
  seventh chords (Module 8) rather than immediately after cadences (Module 7), even
  though form technically only needs cadence recognition. This keeps all harmonic-label
  content (triads through sevenths) contiguous before the analytical lens shifts to
  structure, on the view that switching between "more chord vocabulary" and "zoom out
  to structure" and back would be more disorienting than finishing the vocabulary arc
  first. A reader who disagrees could swap Modules 8 and 9 without breaking any
  prerequisite, since Module 9 only actually depends on Modules 6 and 7.
