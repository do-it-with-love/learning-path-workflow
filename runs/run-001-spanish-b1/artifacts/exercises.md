# Exercises — Conversational European Spanish for Travel

## Summary
Speaking is the constraint this practice plan is built around, not an add-on: every
module opens with a short recurring spoken warm-up done at the start of each of that
module's sessions (retrieval from memory, not review), and every module closes with a
recorded, self-checkable production task — a monologue, role-play, or narration — so
the learner produces spoken language on their own in every single week, not just at
milestones. Distinción and vosotros are drilled from module 1 onward and re-surface in
every later warm-up and synthesis task rather than being taught once and dropped.
Total designed practice is **~23.25 hours across 26 weeks** (29 exercises over 9
modules), averaging under an hour a week but concentrated in short, frequent,
retrieval-heavy sessions rather than long blocks — this sits comfortably inside the
roughly 1.75–2 h/week that remains after resource viewing (60–65% of the 5 h/week
budget), leaving deliberate slack for weeks where a resource runs long, for the
assessment checkpoints, and for life. Module weeks below are apportioned from the
curriculum's module hours (roughly 26 × module_hours / 108, rounded), with module 8
getting a lighter 2-week, 6-session allocation matching its smaller content load;
`schedule-planner` owns the final week-by-week placement and may adjust this without
changing the exercises themselves. Recurring drills use rotation (a different verb,
sound pair, or scenario each session) rather than pure repetition, since production
mechanics need many discrete retrieval attempts, not one long drill.

## Findings

### Module 1: Sounds, Script & Survival Basics
*(Pre-A1 → A1.1, 3 weeks / 9 sessions)*

**Drill — Alternating Sound & Recall Warm-Up (recurring)**
- Task: At the start of each of this module's 9 sessions, spend 10 minutes on a
  rotating warm-up. Odd sessions: record yourself saying 6 words that contrast the
  Peninsular distinción and other key sounds — e.g. zapato/sapo, cielo/seis,
  cerveza/casa (c/z vs s), perro/pero (rr vs r), jamón, año — then play the recording
  back and listen for an audible difference. Even sessions: recite the full subject
  pronoun set including vosotros, count 0–20, and run through a short greeting
  exchange (¡Hola!/¿Qué tal?/Adiós), all from memory, no notes.
- Success criteria: By session 9, every distinción/rr word has an audibly different
  consonant on playback, and you complete the pronoun set, the count to 20, and the
  greeting exchange without pausing more than 2 seconds on any single item.
- Time: 10 min × 9 sessions = 90 min total.
- When: first 10 minutes of every session, weeks 1–3.
- If stuck: halve the set (3 words, or half the pronoun/number list) rather than
  skipping the warm-up; if the rolled rr won't come, substitute a light double-tap of
  the tongue tip and flag it as an ongoing target rather than blocking on it.

**Application — Read-Aloud & Self-Introduction Role-Play**
- Task: Find or write a short unfamiliar Spanish text (a sign, menu snippet, or a few
  sentences using this module's vocabulary) and sound it out aloud using letter-sound
  rules, even for words whose meaning you don't know. Then record a 45–60 second
  self-introduction addressed to an imagined group, using at least one correct
  vosotros form, giving your name and where you're from.
- Success criteria: You sound out every word in the text without stalling more than 3
  seconds; your recorded introduction includes one correct vosotros form and, by the
  second take, lasts 45+ seconds without reading word-for-word from a script.
- Time: 30 min.
- When: week 2, weekend session.
- If stuck: simplify the text to isolated words or short phrases; script the
  introduction first, record reading it, then attempt once from memory.

**Stretch (optional) — Alphabet Speed Spell**
- Task: Spell your own name and three Spanish place names (e.g. Madrid, Sevilla,
  Zaragoza) aloud using the Spanish alphabet, timed. Repeat once and try to beat your
  first time.
- Success criteria: Second attempt is faster than the first with no letter-name
  errors.
- Time: 10 min.
- When: week 3, any session with spare time.
- If stuck: practise letter names in groups of 5 before attempting full words.

### Module 2: Being & Describing
*(A1.1 → A1.2, 3 weeks / 9 sessions)*

**Drill — Ser/Estar & Agreement Warm-Up (recurring)**
- Task: At the start of each of this module's 9 sessions, 10 minutes: pick 2 nouns you
  haven't used yet and say each with a correctly agreeing article + adjective, then
  build and speak 2 fresh sentences — one with ser, one with estar — rotating topic
  each session (nationality, mood, profession, location, appearance, weather) so
  content doesn't repeat.
- Success criteria: By the last session, you produce a correct ser sentence and a
  correct estar sentence within 10 seconds of picking a topic, with no gender/number
  mismatches on playback.
- Time: 10 min × 9 sessions = 90 min total.
- When: first 10 minutes of every session, weeks 1–3.
- If stuck: drill only one of ser/estar in a given session rather than both; write the
  sentence first if speaking cold isn't working, then say it once written.

**Application — Introduce Yourself and a Friend**
- Task: Record a 60–90 second monologue introducing yourself and one other person
  (real or invented) to an imagined group, covering name, origin, one trait each with
  ser, and current mood/location with estar. You decide the register (formal/casual)
  and note in one sentence afterward why you chose it.
- Success criteria: The recording contains 2+ correct ser uses, 2+ correct estar uses,
  correct agreement throughout, and one vosotros-directed line; you can justify your
  register choice in one sentence.
- Time: 30 min.
- When: week 2, weekend session.
- If stuck: script it first, mark each ser/estar choice, perform from the script, then
  simplify to describing only yourself if a second person is too much.

**Synthesis — Sounds + Description Combo**
- Task: Re-record your module-1 self-introduction, now adding two full descriptive
  sentences (ser/estar, correct agreement) about yourself and one other person, while
  consciously applying the distinción and rr from module 1 as you speak.
- Success criteria: You can point to at least one moment on playback where you
  audibly corrected a module-1 pronunciation point while producing module-2 grammar.
- Time: 20 min.
- When: week 3, weekend session.
- If stuck: do the pronunciation pass and the grammar pass separately first, then
  merge them on a third take.

### Module 3: Present-Tense Action
*(A1.2 → A1.3, 3 weeks / 9 sessions)*

**Drill — Conjugation & Question Rotation (recurring)**
- Task: At the start of each of this module's 9 sessions, 10 minutes: conjugate one
  verb through all 6 persons including vosotros (rotate through the regular -ar/-er/-ir
  verbs and tener/ir/querer/poder/hacer across the 9 sessions so each is covered at
  least once), then invent and answer 2 questions aloud using a different question
  word each session (qué/dónde/cuándo/cómo/quién, cycling through).
- Success criteria: By session 9 all 8 target verbs and all 5 question words have been
  drilled at least once; by the module's second half you complete a session's
  conjugation and questions within the 10 minutes without notes.
- Time: 10 min × 9 sessions = 90 min total.
- When: first 10 minutes of every session, weeks 1–3.
- If stuck: drop to 3 persons instead of 6, and 1 question instead of 2, in a given
  session; catch up the skipped forms later in the module.

**Application — Unscripted Q&A With Yourself**
- Task: Record yourself asking 8 personal questions covering all 5 question words and
  a mix of tener/querer/hacer/ir/poder and regular verbs, then switch roles and answer
  each one aloud in character, without pre-writing the answers.
- Success criteria: All 8 answers use a correctly conjugated verb matching the
  appropriate person; by the second take you don't pause more than 3 seconds mid-answer.
- Time: 30 min.
- When: week 2, weekend session.
- If stuck: prepare just the verb form for each answer beforehand, then improvise the
  rest of the sentence live.

**Synthesis — Meet-and-Greet Chain**
- Task: Combine modules 1–3 in one continuous 90-second recording: greet someone using
  vosotros-appropriate language, describe yourself and them with ser/estar and correct
  agreement, then ask and answer 3 personal questions using present-tense action verbs
  — no stopping to restart.
- Success criteria: The recording runs start to finish without a restart and contains
  one vosotros form, one ser, one estar, correct agreement, and 3 correctly conjugated
  action-verb answers.
- Time: 30 min.
- When: week 3, weekend session.
- If stuck: record each of the three parts separately first, then attempt the full
  chain once each part works alone.

### Module 4: Daily Life & Routine
*(A1.3 → A2.1, 3 weeks / 9 sessions)*

**Drill — Routine, Time & Gustar Warm-Up (recurring)**
- Task: At the start of each of this module's 9 sessions, 10 minutes: conjugate one
  reflexive verb in yo and vosotros (rotate levantarse/ducharse/vestirse/acostarse/
  despertarse across sessions), state the current time and date aloud, and give one
  gustar-type opinion about something new each session.
- Success criteria: By session 9 every target reflexive verb has been drilled at least
  twice; you state time, date, and a gustar opinion within the 10 minutes without more
  than a few seconds' hesitation per item.
- Time: 10 min × 9 sessions = 90 min total.
- When: first 10 minutes of every session, weeks 1–3.
- If stuck: drop vosotros temporarily and drill only yo until automatic, then reintroduce
  vosotros.

**Application — Narrate Your Actual Day**
- Task: At the end of a real day, record a spoken narration (8+ sentences, at least 1
  minute) of what you actually did, in chronological order, using 3+ reflexive verbs,
  a correct time expression, and one gustar-type opinion about the day. Repeat with a
  different real day later in the module.
- Success criteria: Each recording is 1+ minute / 8+ sentences with 3+ correctly
  conjugated reflexive verbs, a time expression, and a gustar statement.
- Time: 20 min per occurrence × 2 occurrences = 40 min total.
- When: one weekday evening in week 2, and again in week 3.
- If stuck: list your day's actions in English as bullets first, translate each, then
  perform from the translated list.

**Synthesis — The Full Day, In Character**
- Task: Combine modules 2–4 in one continuous 2-minute recording: introduce who you
  are (ser/estar), describe your typical routine (reflexives, time), and include
  asking and answering one question about someone else's routine using present-tense
  action verbs.
- Success criteria: All three grammar layers appear, correctly formed, in a single
  continuous 2-minute take.
- Time: 30 min.
- When: week 3, weekend session.
- If stuck: record the three layers on separate days earlier in the week, then attempt
  one final continuous take.

### Module 5: Getting Around
*(A2.1, 3 weeks / 9 sessions)*

**Drill — Directions Warm-Up (recurring)**
- Task: At the start of each of this module's 9 sessions, 10 minutes: invent a fresh
  4-step mini route from memory (e.g. from your front door to somewhere nearby) using
  imperative direction commands (gira, sigue, cruza, para, dobla) and at least 2
  different prepositions of place each time (a la derecha, a la izquierda, todo recto,
  al lado de, enfrente de, entre).
- Success criteria: Correct imperative endings and 2+ distinct prepositions per route;
  by the module's second half you do this without notes.
- Time: 10 min × 9 sessions = 90 min total.
- When: first 10 minutes of every session, weeks 1–3.
- If stuck: write the route in English first, map each step to Spanish, then speak it;
  drop to 2 steps if 4 is too much.

**Application — Read a Sign, Then Ask**
- Task: Find or write a short piece of transport/travel signage (a departure line, a
  street sign, a simple notice), read it aloud and explain its meaning in 1–2 Spanish
  sentences. Then record yourself asking for directions to a place named in the sign
  and improvising a reply as if a stranger answered.
- Success criteria: You correctly explain the sign's meaning aloud; the direction
  request uses correct question form and the improvised reply uses 2+ imperative
  commands.
- Time: 25 min.
- When: week 2, weekday session.
- If stuck: translate the sign to English for your own understanding first, then
  re-attempt explaining it in Spanish only.

**Application — Shadow a Natural-Pace Exchange**
- Task: Using any short (30–90 second) Peninsular Spanish audio or video clip about
  directions or transport that you can access, listen once for gist, then shadow it
  (speak along a half-beat behind the speaker) three times, and finally record
  yourself reproducing 3 phrases from it from memory with the audio off.
- Success criteria: By the third shadowing pass you keep pace without falling more
  than a word behind; you reproduce 3 phrases from memory with recognisable
  pronunciation on playback.
- Time: 20 min.
- When: week 2, weekend session.
- If stuck: slow the clip's playback speed, or shadow one sentence at a time instead
  of the whole clip.

**Synthesis — Lost in Town Role-Play**
- Task: Combine modules 1–4 in one continuous 90-second recording playing both a lost
  traveller (asking directions politely, mentioning their day's plan) and a local
  giving directions with imperatives and prepositions, switching voice for each role.
- Success criteria: Both roles appear in one continuous recording; direction-giving
  uses 3+ imperatives, and the traveller role includes a correctly conjugated
  present-tense verb from an earlier module.
- Time: 30 min.
- When: week 3, weekend session.
- If stuck: write a two-column script (traveller line / local line), perform reading
  it, then attempt once from memory.

### Module 6: Eating, Shopping & Lodging
*(A2.1 → A2.2, 3 weeks / 9 sessions)*

**Drill — Comparatives & Polite Requests Warm-Up (recurring)**
- Task: At the start of each of this module's 9 sessions, 10 minutes: say 2 fresh
  comparative sentences (más/menos...que) about prices or sizes of everyday items, and
  1 polite request using a different pattern each session (¿Podría...?, Me
  gustaría..., ¿Me pone...?).
- Success criteria: Correct que placement and adjective agreement in the comparatives;
  a complete, natural-sounding request each time.
- Time: 10 min × 9 sessions = 90 min total.
- When: first 10 minutes of every session, weeks 1–3.
- If stuck: pre-write one comparative and one request the night before if speaking
  cold isn't working yet, then perform them from memory in the session.

**Application — Read Then Order**
- Task: Write or find a short menu (5–6 items with prices) or a simple hotel notice,
  read it aloud, then record yourself placing a food order or checking into lodging
  based on it, including one polite request and one comparative statement about two
  options.
- Success criteria: The recorded order/check-in is understandable start to finish
  without long pauses, and includes 1 polite form and 1 correctly used comparative.
- Time: 30 min.
- When: week 2, weekend session.
- If stuck: script the interaction, perform from the script twice, then attempt
  unscripted.

**Synthesis — Full Travel Day**
- Task: Combine modules 1, 4, 5 and 6 in one continuous 2–3 minute recording: greet
  and describe yourself, ask for directions to a restaurant or hotel, then order food
  or check in using polite forms and one comparative.
- Success criteria: One continuous take covering all three stages, with 5+ distinct,
  correctly formed grammar points drawn from modules 1–6 (list which afterward as a
  self-check).
- Time: 35 min.
- When: week 3, weekend session.
- If stuck: record each of the three stages separately earlier in the week, then
  combine on the final attempt.

### Module 7: Talking About the Past
*(A2.2, 3 weeks / 9 sessions)*

**Drill — Pretérito Warm-Up (recurring)**
- Task: At the start of each of this module's 9 sessions, 10 minutes: conjugate one
  pretérito verb through all 6 persons (rotate through 3 regulars — one each of
  -ar/-er/-ir — and the 5 irregulars ir, ser, hacer, tener, estar across the 9
  sessions), then convert 2 present-tense sentences you say aloud into pretérito on
  the spot.
- Success criteria: By session 9 all 8 verbs have been drilled at least once; by the
  module's second half, present-to-past conversions are correct without checking
  notes.
- Time: 10 min × 9 sessions = 90 min total.
- When: first 10 minutes of every session, weeks 1–3.
- If stuck: drill only the irregulars first since they carry most of the weight;
  return to regulars once irregulars are solid.

**Application — Narrate a Real Past Weekend**
- Task: Record a spoken narration (4–6 connected sentences minimum, aim for 8–10) of
  something you actually did recently, using pretérito for the sequence of events and
  present tense only for a current opinion about it now. Repeat with a different
  memory later in the module.
- Success criteria: The narration correctly uses pretérito for all sequenced actions
  (2+ irregular forms included) and doesn't drift into present tense for the narrated
  events themselves.
- Time: 25 min per occurrence × 2 occurrences = 50 min total.
- When: one evening in week 2, and again in week 3.
- If stuck: write the events as an English timeline first, translate each to
  pretérito, then perform.

**Synthesis — Trip Story Chain**
- Task: Combine modules 4, 6 and 7 in one continuous 2-minute recording: where you
  went (pretérito of ir), what you did there (pretérito action verbs), what you ate or
  bought (comparatives/quantities), and how you feel about it now (present-tense
  opinion).
- Success criteria: The story moves correctly between pretérito for events and present
  tense for reflection, and includes at least one grammar point each from modules 4, 6
  and 7.
- Time: 30 min.
- When: week 3, weekend session.
- If stuck: outline the 4 required elements as bullets, draft one sentence per bullet,
  perform from the outline, then attempt unscripted.

### Module 8: Plans, Requests & Unexpected Situations
*(A2.2 → A2.3, 2 weeks / 6 sessions)*

**Drill — Near-Future Warm-Up (recurring)**
- Task: At the start of each of this module's 6 sessions, 10 minutes: say 2 fresh ir a
  + infinitive plans about things you're actually going to do, rotating yo, vosotros,
  and another person across sessions so vosotros appears at least twice overall.
- Success criteria: Correct ir conjugation plus a bare infinitive every time; vosotros
  used correctly at least twice across the module.
- Time: 10 min × 6 sessions = 60 min total.
- When: first 10 minutes of every session, weeks 1–2.
- If stuck: use only the yo form for a session, adding other persons back the next
  session.

**Application — Report a Problem**
- Task: Record a 60–90 second improvised exchange reporting a problem or asking for
  help in a service/emergency-adjacent scenario (lost item, delayed transport, needing
  a pharmacy, a booking issue — pick one), playing both your own role and an imagined
  responder, at as natural a pace as you can manage.
- Success criteria: You state the problem clearly in the first 2 sentences, ask for
  help using a question form from an earlier module, and the recording is not a
  scripted read (hesitation is fine; reading a full script is not).
- Time: 25 min.
- When: week 1, weekend session.
- If stuck: slow your speech and allow longer pauses rather than switching to
  English; if truly stuck, write 3 key prompt sentences (not a full script).

**Synthesis — Invite, Plan, and Confirm**
- Task: Combine modules 3, 6 and 7 in one continuous 90-second recording: invite
  someone using ir a + infinitive, have them ask a question about the plan (module-3
  forms), answer it referencing something you did last time (pretérito, module 7), and
  confirm a time/place (module 4/6 vocabulary).
- Success criteria: All four elements — invitation, question, past reference,
  confirmation — appear in one continuous recording with correct tense use throughout.
- Time: 30 min.
- When: week 2, weekend session.
- If stuck: draft the four required lines in English, translate one at a time,
  rehearse each separately, then combine.

### Module 9: Conversation Consolidation
*(A2.3 → B1-threshold, 3 weeks / 9 sessions)*

**Drill — Self-Correction Loop (recurring)**
- Task: At the start of each of this module's 9 sessions, 10 minutes: speak freely for
  60 seconds on any topic covered in this path, replay the recording and mark every
  ser/estar, agreement, or verb-ending error you hear, then re-record the same 60
  seconds once, deliberately fixing what you marked.
- Success criteria: The second take each session has fewer marked errors than the
  first; by session 9 you can name specific error types you've stopped making.
- Time: 10 min × 9 sessions = 90 min total.
- When: first 10 minutes of every session, weeks 1–3.
- If stuck: transcribe one sentence in writing if you can't yet hear your own errors
  by ear.

**Application — Listening Without a Transcript**
- Task: Find or replay a Peninsular Spanish audio/video clip (60–120 seconds) covering
  a travel scenario (transport, restaurant, shop, or emergency-adjacent) you haven't
  already studied closely. Listen once without pausing, then summarize aloud in
  Spanish (3–4 sentences) what happened, without reading anything. Repeat with a
  different scenario type later in the module.
- Success criteria: Your spoken summary correctly identifies the scenario and 2+
  concrete details, produced without pausing the clip or reading a transcript first.
- Time: 20 min per occurrence × 2 occurrences = 40 min total.
- When: one weekday session in week 1, another in week 2, each covering a different
  scenario.
- If stuck: allow one pause partway through on a repeat attempt, or drop to
  identifying just the scenario and one detail.

**Synthesis — The Full 3–5 Minute Conversation**
- Task: This is the module's centrepiece. Record a solo 3–5 minute spoken
  "conversation" playing both sides of an exchange covering: an introduction, your
  daily routine, and at least two travel scenarios (e.g. transport and a restaurant),
  mixing present tense and pretérito correctly, using vosotros at least once, and
  audibly self-correcting at least one error ("digo... quiero decir..."). Do a shorter
  2-minute dry run first as rehearsal.
- Success criteria: The final recording reaches 3–5 minutes without long dead
  silences, covers all required elements, and you can list afterward which module each
  piece came from.
- Time: 20 min (2-minute dry run) + 40 min (final take) = 60 min total.
- When: dry run in week 2, weekend session; final take in week 3, weekend session.
- If stuck: build it from pieces you already recorded in modules 4–8 — reuse and
  stitch your own past recordings rather than starting from nothing; cut the target to
  2 minutes / 1 scenario if needed, then extend on a later attempt.

**Stretch (optional) — Cold Q&A**
- Task: Write 8 unpredictable personal/travel questions on slips of paper the day
  before, shuffle them, then draw and answer each aloud on the spot the next day so
  you can't pre-plan answers. Use a language-exchange partner instead if one is
  available.
- Success criteria: You answer 6+ of 8 questions with a grammatically complete Spanish
  sentence within 10 seconds of drawing the question.
- Time: 20 min.
- When: week 3, any extra session.
- If stuck: reduce to 5 questions or allow 20 seconds instead of 10.

## Open Questions
- **Self-checkability of pronunciation and register.** A learner can hear whether two
  words sound different on playback, but cannot reliably judge whether their
  distinción, rr trill, or overall accent would pass as natural to a native Peninsular
  ear, and the module 8 "Report a Problem" exercise cannot self-verify whether the
  register/urgency actually sounds natural rather than just grammatically correct. A
  tutor or native speaker check-in at the end of modules 1, 3, and 8 would catch drift
  that self-review cannot.
- **Module 9's final conversation is judged by the learner alone.** The success
  criteria (elements present, length, one audible self-correction) are checkable
  without help, but genuine conversational fluency — whether a real Spaniard could
  follow and respond naturally at that pace — is not something a solo recording can
  confirm. If a language exchange or tutor session is available near the end of the
  path, this module is where it would add the most value; the optional Cold Q&A
  stretch is a partial substitute, not a replacement.
- **Module weeks are apportioned from curriculum module hours, not fixed by this
  artifact.** Weeks-per-module (3/3/3/3/3/3/3/2/3) approximate the curriculum's
  relative module sizes; `schedule-planner` owns exact placement and may compress or
  extend a module's span, which would change how the recurring per-session drills are
  distributed without changing their total time.
