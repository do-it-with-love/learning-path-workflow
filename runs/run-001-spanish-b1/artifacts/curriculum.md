# Curriculum — Conversational European Spanish for Travel

## Summary
This path has **9 modules**, no capstone (the goal names a capability — holding
travel conversations — not a concrete deliverable), sized to **108 hours** of module
content against a **130-hour budget** (5h/week × 26 weeks), leaving **22 hours (~17%)**
of slack for revision, life, and the assessment checkpoints `assessment-designer` will
add on top. Progression follows the real shape of a beginner Spanish syllabus —
pronunciation and script, then ser/estar and agreement, then present-tense action verbs,
then the situational blocks a traveller actually needs (routine, transport, food/lodging),
then past tense, then near-future/plans — with fine-grained CEFR sub-levels (A1.1 through
A2.3/B1-threshold) so no module jumps more than one step past the one before it. Because
the learner's baseline is **pre-A1** with no confirmed Peninsular exposure (per
`baseline-assessment.md`), Peninsular pronunciation (distinción /θ/ vs /s/) and
**vosotros** are built into modules 1–3 as core content, not a later add-on. Every module
from 2 onward carries an explicit spoken-production objective — describing, narrating,
role-playing, asking/answering — so speaking is exercised continuously rather than
bolted on at the end; module 9 is a consolidation of spoken conversation across all
scenarios named in the target outcomes, not a new grammar unit. Deliberately left out:
imperfect tense, subjunctive mood, compound (perfect) tenses, formal/business register,
sustained writing practice, and Latin American dialectal forms — none of which the
confirmed target outcomes require, and reintroducing them would burn hours the honest
130-hour budget does not have.

### Deliberately excluded
A fuller B1/certified-B1 treatment would additionally include: the imperfect tense
(needed for extended past-tense narrative and habitual-past description); the
subjunctive mood (needed for wishes, doubt, and formal requests); compound/perfect
tenses (haber + participle); sustained written composition and formal register;
extended vocabulary beyond the ~1000-word travel/social core (e.g. workplace, abstract,
media Spanish); and any Latin American dialectal forms (ustedes-only address, seseo as
a default, voseo), which are explicitly out of scope per the retargeted, Peninsular-only
goal. These are excluded because the confirmed target outcomes in `requirements.md` do
not require them and the honest 130-hour budget (already ~a third of certified-B1 hours)
has no slack to add them without displacing the speaking practice the learner asked for.

## Open Questions
- **Interpretation of gate G9's "one level above baseline" rule.** Read literally against
  the single pre-A1 baseline, every module would be capped at A1 — which would make it
  impossible to reach the confirmed A2/B1-threshold target at all. I have instead applied
  it as "one sub-step above the level established by the immediately preceding module,"
  using fine-grained CEFR sub-levels (A1.1 → A1.2 → A1.3 → A2.1 → A2.2 → A2.3 →
  B1-threshold) so that no module ever assumes more than one step of progress beyond what
  the prior module taught. If `validator` intends the stricter literal reading, the target
  outcomes as stated (A2/B1-threshold) are not reachable under G9 within any number of
  modules, which would be a conflict between G9 and the requirements' retargeted goal —
  flagging this explicitly rather than silently picking one interpretation.
- **Module 9 sizing.** At 13 hours across 3 weeks of the schedule it is the largest single
  consolidation block; if `schedule-planner` finds this too dense for spaced practice, it
  could be split into two shorter modules (e.g. "Listening consolidation" and "Speaking
  consolidation") without changing total hours — flagged here rather than pre-decided,
  since splitting is a scheduling call, not a content one.
- **Order of modules 5 and 6.** Getting Around (transport/directions) is placed before
  Eating, Shopping & Lodging on the reasoning that a traveller needs to arrive before they
  order or check in, but the two are largely independent of each other (both depend only
  on modules 1–4) and could be swapped without breaking any prerequisite chain.
