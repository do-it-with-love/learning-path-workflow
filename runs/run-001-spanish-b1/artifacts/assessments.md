# Assessments — Conversational European Spanish for Travel

## Summary
Every module gets one self-scored checkpoint (20–30 minutes) built around *producing*
speech, writing, or comprehension answers rather than recognising the right multiple-choice
option — because the target outcomes in `requirements.md` are spoken, a checkpoint that can
be passed by someone who only recognises correct Spanish is worthless. Three cumulative
reviews (after modules 3, 6, and 9) add short retrieval passes over everything so far, and
a final check maps directly onto all five target outcomes, including a self-audit against
the 800–1000 word active-vocabulary target. Total assessment time across the whole path is
about 5.5 hours (~325 minutes) against the ~22-hour slack `curriculum.md` reports over its
108 hours of module content — assessment is a small, deliberate tax on that slack, not a
competitor for it. The hardest honest limitation of this design: self-scoring cannot detect
what the learner cannot hear in their own speech, which is exactly true of the two Peninsular
targets (distinción and rr) and of real-time listening comprehension against unpredictable
native speech — both are flagged in Open Questions rather than assessed with a check that
would just give false confidence. Rubric bands throughout are calibrated to a self-taught
beginner's real trajectory: in modules 1–4, landing in *good enough to continue* is the
expected, on-track result, not a shortfall — *solid* is a bonus, not the bar.

## Findings

### How to self-score speech (used throughout)
Three repeatable techniques, referenced by short name in the checkpoints below:
- **Record-and-transcribe**: record the spoken task, then write out exactly what you said
  (not what you meant to say), and mark it against the checklist for that checkpoint.
- **Shadow-and-compare**: play a short native Peninsular audio/video clip from your course
  materials phrase by phrase, immediately repeat each phrase aloud and record yourself, then
  play your recording and the native clip back to back and mark each target sound
  pass/fail.
- **Pre-commit answer key**: before recording, jot the conjugations/vocabulary you intend to
  use; after recording, check what you actually said against that key rather than against
  memory, which is unreliable for your own errors.

### Module 1: Sounds, Script & Survival Basics
**Check (20 min).** Record yourself doing four things back to back: (1) recite the alphabet;
(2) say 10 words chosen to contain ñ, rr (double-r or word-initial r), j, and c/z-before-e/i
(for distinción); (3) count aloud 0–20; (4) deliver a 30-second greeting/introduction aimed
at a group, using a vosotros-directed sentence at least once ("¿Cómo estáis?" or similar).
Use shadow-and-compare against a native Peninsular clip for the 10-word list, then
record-and-transcribe the greeting.
**Rubric.**
- *Not yet*: more than 2 of the 10 target sounds are consistently wrong (rr indistinguishable
  from single r, distinción absent throughout, j pronounced as English j or h-less); alphabet
  or 0–20 count has long pauses or requires notes; no vosotros form attempted.
- *Good enough to continue*: alphabet and 0–20 recited fluently without notes; at least 8 of
  10 target-sound words match the native clip on shadow-and-compare; distinción attempted
  consistently even if not always accurate; vosotros form produced correctly at least once.
- *Solid*: all 10 target-sound words match the native clip including distinción in connected
  (not just isolated-word) speech; no hesitation on alphabet/numbers; vosotros used without
  prompting.
**Go/no-go.** *Not yet* on alphabet/numbers or on more than half the target sounds: redo the
pronunciation and phoneme objective's drills, re-shadow the native clip three more times, and
repeat the checkpoint before starting module 2 — this module is foundation for every spoken
checkpoint after it. *Not yet* specifically and only on distinción: proceed, but flag it for
extra shadow practice in every later module's checkpoint until it clears, since it is the
hardest Peninsular sound to self-detect (see Open Questions).

### Module 2: Being & Describing
**Check (20 min).** Record a 60–90 second monologue: describe yourself (name, origin, two
traits) and one other person, real or invented, addressing an imagined pair of people in one
sentence using vosotros. Record-and-transcribe, then mark every ser/estar choice and every
adjective agreement against a checklist: correct verb, correct reason (identity/characteristic
vs. state/location), correct gender/number match.
**Rubric.**
- *Not yet*: ser/estar wrong or unexplainable in 3+ instances; most adjectives fail to agree
  in gender or number; vosotros not attempted or malformed.
- *Good enough to continue*: ser/estar correct in most instances, with at most 1–2 slips you
  can explain on review; simple adjective agreement (gender and number) correct on review;
  vosotros form correct at least once.
- *Solid*: ser/estar correct throughout, including cases where the same adjective changes
  meaning with the two verbs (e.g. es aburrido vs. está aburrido); agreement automatic, no
  errors found on transcription; vosotros used naturally, more than once.
**Go/no-go.** *Not yet*: revisit the ser/estar and agreement objectives, redo the drills for
whichever half failed (ser/estar or agreement), and re-record on a new topic (a different
person) before moving to module 3, since module 3's question-and-answer checkpoint assumes
these are solid enough not to be the bottleneck.

### Module 3: Present-Tense Action
**Check (25 min).** Prepare only a list of 8 questions (2 each with qué/dónde/cuándo/cómo,
mixing regular verbs and tener, ir, querer, poder, hacer) — not the answers. Record yourself
asking and then answering each aloud, unscripted. Record-and-transcribe, then mark: correct
question word order, correct conjugation for each verb used (regular and the 5 irregulars),
and whether the answer was produced without rereading the prepared question.
**Rubric.**
- *Not yet*: 3+ conjugation errors on review, question word order wrong more than twice, or
  answers required rereading/rehearsing before speaking.
- *Good enough to continue*: at most 2 conjugation slips across the 8 exchanges, question
  word order consistently correct, answers produced with only brief (1–2 second) hesitation.
- *Solid*: all 8 exchanges conjugated correctly including the irregulars, no hesitation, and
  you can vary the answer if asked the same question a second way.
**Go/no-go.** *Not yet*: redo the irregular-verb drills specifically for whichever of the 5
verbs failed, and re-run the checkpoint with a fresh set of 8 questions before the cumulative
review below — carrying unfixed irregular-verb errors into the review will just relabel the
same gap as "forgotten" three modules later.

### Cumulative review — after Module 3 (25 min)
A closed-notes retrieval pass over modules 1–3, self-timed. Without looking anything up: (1)
recite the alphabet and count 0–30; (2) write 5 sentences describing people/things using
ser/estar correctly; (3) conjugate tener, ir, querer, poder, hacer in first and second-person
plural (vosotros) present tense from memory; (4) say one full vosotros-directed sentence
aloud. Mark against a self-made answer key assembled from your own correct answers in modules
1–3's checkpoints (keep those transcripts). Score each of the four parts pass/fail.
**Go/no-go.** Any part fails: that module's checkpoint content, not just this review, is the
gap — return to the relevant module's checkpoint go/no-go rule above and redo it before
continuing to module 4. Passing all four is the signal that modules 1–3 are load-bearing
enough to build present-tense fluency (module 4) on top of.

### Module 4: Daily Life & Routine
**Check (20 min).** Record a 60-second narration of a typical day using reflexive verbs, at
least one time expression and one gustar-type sentence. Record-and-transcribe; mark reflexive
verb conjugation (including the reflexive pronoun matching the subject), time expressions,
and gustar agreement (gusta vs. gustan) against a checklist.
**Rubric.**
- *Not yet*: reflexive pronoun missing or mismatched in most sentences, no clear time
  expressions used, gustar-type verbs avoided or consistently wrong.
- *Good enough to continue*: reflexive verbs correctly formed in most of the narration, at
  least 2 time expressions used correctly, gustar/gustan matched to its subject at least once
  correctly.
- *Solid*: reflexive verbs automatic and correct throughout, several time expressions woven
  in naturally, gustar-type agreement correct every time including with plural subjects.
**Go/no-go.** *Not yet*: revisit the reflexive-verb and gustar objectives, redo with a
different routine (e.g. a weekend instead of a weekday) before module 5, since Getting
Around's role-play assumes fluent narration as a base skill.

### Module 5: Getting Around
**Check (25 min).** Two parts. (a) Speaking: play both roles of a directions exchange aloud —
one asking for directions to a place, one giving them using imperatives and prepositions of
place — and record it. Record-and-transcribe; mark imperative form and preposition accuracy.
(b) Listening + reading: shadow-and-compare a short native Peninsular clip about transport or
directions at slow-to-natural pace (no transcript on first pass), then answer 3
self-written comprehension questions about it before checking the transcript; separately,
read any short transport sign or timetable text you can find and answer 2 questions about it
against your own answer key.
**Rubric.**
- *Not yet*: imperatives malformed or avoided, prepositions wrong in most instances, fewer
  than 2 of 5 comprehension/reading questions answered correctly before checking transcript.
- *Good enough to continue*: imperatives correctly formed for at least 3 of the directions
  given, prepositions mostly correct, 3–4 of 5 comprehension/reading questions correct before
  checking the transcript.
- *Solid*: imperatives and prepositions correct throughout, all 5 comprehension/reading
  questions correct before checking the transcript, and you can restate the directions in
  your own words without rereading.
**Go/no-go.** *Not yet* on speaking: redo the imperative/preposition drills and re-record.
*Not yet* on listening specifically: re-shadow the clip at half speed if available, then at
full speed, before retrying the 3 questions — do not proceed to module 6's listening-heavy
checkpoint with an unresolved listening gap.

### Module 6: Eating, Shopping & Lodging
**Check (25 min).** Record a role-play, playing both sides: ordering food at a restaurant and
checking into lodging, including at least one polite request form (e.g. ¿Podría...?, Me
gustaría...) and one comparative (más/menos... que). Record-and-transcribe; mark comparative
formation, polite request form, and money/quantity expressions against a checklist.
Separately, read a menu, shop sign, or hotel notice (any real one you can find) and answer 3
self-written questions about it before checking your own key.
**Rubric.**
- *Not yet*: polite request forms avoided or ungrammatical, comparatives malformed, fewer
  than 2 of 3 reading questions answered correctly.
- *Good enough to continue*: at least one correct polite request form used, comparative
  correct at least once, 2–3 of 3 reading questions correct.
- *Solid*: polite forms and comparatives correct and used more than once each, all reading
  questions correct, and the role-play flows without long pauses between the customer and
  staff "turns."
**Go/no-go.** *Not yet*: revisit polite-request and comparative objectives, redo the role-play
with a different scenario (e.g. shop instead of restaurant) before the module 6 cumulative
review below.

### Cumulative review — after Module 6 (25 min)
Closed-notes retrieval over modules 1–6. Without looking anything up: (1) narrate your daily
routine in 4–5 sentences using reflexives and at least one gustar-type verb; (2) give
directions to an imagined location using an imperative and a preposition; (3) order a meal
aloud including one polite request form; (4) recite the distinción-relevant word list from
module 1 once more, shadow-comparing against the same native clip used then, to check the
sound hasn't drifted or been forgotten. Self-score each of the four parts pass/fail against
transcripts from the original checkpoints.
**Go/no-go.** Any part fails: return to that module's checkpoint go/no-go rule and redo it.
Pay particular attention to part 4 — pronunciation is the skill most likely to quietly slide
backward without deliberate re-practice, and there is no later checkpoint dedicated to
catching that on its own.

### Module 7: Talking About the Past
**Check (20 min).** Using the pre-commit answer key technique, write out (do not say yet) the
correct pretérito conjugations you intend to use for ir, ser, hacer, tener, estar and two
regular verbs in a 4–6 sentence account of a past trip or weekend. Then record yourself
narrating it aloud without reading the written key. Compare the recording (via
record-and-transcribe) against your pre-committed key; mark each verb correct/incorrect, and
separately mark whether pretérito (not present tense) was used throughout.
**Rubric.**
- *Not yet*: 3+ verbs wrong against your own key, or present tense used instead of pretérito
  in most sentences.
- *Good enough to continue*: at most 1–2 verbs wrong against your key, pretérito correctly
  chosen over present tense in most of the narrative.
- *Solid*: all verbs match your key including the 5 irregulars, pretérito used correctly
  throughout with no present-tense slips, and the narrative holds together as connected
  sentences rather than isolated facts.
**Go/no-go.** *Not yet*: redo the pretérito irregular-verb drills for whichever verbs failed,
and re-narrate a different weekend before module 8 — near-future forms in module 8 are
taught by contrast with past tense, so an unresolved pretérito gap will resurface there.

### Module 8: Plans, Requests & Unexpected Situations
**Check (25 min).** Three short recordings. (a) State 3 near-future plans aloud using ir a +
infinitive; record-and-transcribe and mark the construction (ir conjugated correctly + a +
infinitive, not a conjugated verb). (b) Record yourself handling a mock service or
emergency-adjacent situation (e.g. reporting a lost item, asking for help) as a one-sided
monologue at natural pace; mark against a checklist of whether the core request/problem was
stated clearly and comprehensibly. (c) Shadow-and-compare a native Peninsular clip of a
similar service/emergency scenario at natural pace, then answer 2 self-written comprehension
questions before checking the transcript.
**Rubric.**
- *Not yet*: ir a + infinitive malformed in 2+ of 3 plans, the mock scenario would likely be
  incomprehensible to a listener (you cannot tell, on playback, what the problem/request
  actually was), or 0 of 2 comprehension questions correct before checking transcript.
- *Good enough to continue*: ir a + infinitive correct in at least 2 of 3 plans, the mock
  scenario's core request is clear on playback even with some grammar slips, 1–2 of 2
  comprehension questions correct before checking transcript.
- *Solid*: ir a + infinitive correct in all 3 plans, the mock scenario is fluent and clear
  with natural pacing, both comprehension questions correct before checking transcript.
**Go/no-go.** *Not yet* on (a): redo the near-future drills and re-record. *Not yet* on (b):
this is the outcome that matters most for actual travel use — redo it focusing only on
getting the core message across, even ungrammatically, then re-add grammar on a second take.
*Not yet* on (c): re-shadow the clip; if still failing after a second attempt, proceed but
flag it — natural-pace unscripted listening is the hardest self-assessable skill in this path
and module 9's cumulative review gives one more pass at it.

### Module 9: Conversation Consolidation
**Check (30 min).** Record a 3–5 minute self-interview: prepare a list of prompts covering
introductions, daily routine, and at least two travel scenarios (transport, restaurant, shop,
or lodging), then speak both the prompts and the answers aloud, mixing present and pretérito
as the content requires. Record-and-transcribe the full thing, then mark against a checklist:
ser/estar correct, gender/number agreement correct, verb endings (present and pretérito)
correct, and note every self-correction you made in real time (a self-correction counts as a
partial success, not a failure — it demonstrates the self-monitoring the objective asks for).
Separately, do one shadow-and-compare listening pass on a natural-pace Peninsular clip in a
travel scenario not yet used in this module's checkpoints, without transcript, then check
comprehension against the transcript.
**Rubric.**
- *Not yet*: conversation breaks down before 2 minutes, ser/estar or agreement errors are
  frequent and mostly unnoticed on review, no scenario transitions attempted, listening pass
  comprehension is largely guesswork.
- *Good enough to continue*: conversation sustained close to 3 minutes covering the required
  topics, most grammar errors are ones you catch on transcription review even if not in the
  moment, listening pass gets the gist even if some detail is missed.
- *Solid*: conversation sustained 4–5 minutes with natural topic transitions, errors are rare
  and often self-corrected in real time (audible on the recording), listening pass
  comprehension matches the transcript closely.
**Go/no-go.** *Not yet*: this is the module the whole path has been building toward, so do
not silently pass it — identify which of the four checklist areas (grammar accuracy,
duration/flow, scenario coverage, listening) is weakest, revisit that specific module's
checkpoint go/no-go rule (2–3 for grammar, 5/6/8 for scenario role-play, 1/5/8/9 for
listening), redo that module's drills, and re-run this checkpoint with fresh prompts before
the module 9 cumulative review and final check below.

### Cumulative review — after Module 9 (25 min)
This is a retrieval pass across the *entire* path, distinct from the final check below (which
tests integrated performance against the target outcomes). Without notes: (1) recite the
alphabet, count to 100, and produce 3 distinción-relevant words, shadow-checked against the
module 1 clip one last time; (2) conjugate one regular and one irregular verb in each of
present and pretérito, aloud; (3) form one ir a + infinitive sentence; (4) list 15 vocabulary
items from memory across travel, daily life, and social categories (a preview of the final
check's fuller vocabulary audit). Self-score each part pass/fail against your accumulated
checkpoint transcripts and any vocabulary log you have kept.
**Go/no-go.** Any part fails: this is the last checkpoint before the final check, so treat a
failure here as high-priority — spend one extra study session on that specific area (using
the relevant module's checkpoint materials) before attempting the final check, rather than
attempting the final check with a known gap.

### Final check — full-path assessment (45 min)
Ties directly to the five target outcomes in `requirements.md`. Do all parts in one sitting
where possible, but they can be split across two sessions of the same week.

1. **Spoken conversation (outcome 1).** Record a 3–5 minute conversation (self-interview
   format, as in module 9) that explicitly covers: personal introduction, daily routine, and
   at least two of {ordering food, asking directions, checking into lodging}, using present
   tense throughout and pretérito for at least one past event. Record-and-transcribe.
2. **Listening comprehension (outcome 2).** Shadow-and-compare two natural-pace Peninsular
   clips you have not used in any earlier checkpoint, from two different scenario categories
   among {transport, restaurant, shop, emergency}. Answer 3 self-written comprehension
   questions per clip before checking transcripts.
3. **Grammar in speech (outcome 3).** From the transcript of part 1, mark every instance of:
   present tense conjugation (regular and irregular), ser/estar, pretérito, and ir a +
   infinitive (prompt yourself to include at least one ir a + infinitive sentence if the
   conversation didn't naturally produce one). Tally correct vs. incorrect for each category.
4. **Reading comprehension (outcome 4).** Find one menu, one sign or notice, and one short
   written message in Spanish (any real examples), and answer 2 self-written questions about
   each (6 total) before checking your own answer key.
5. **Active vocabulary (outcome 5).** Two-part self-audit. (a) If you kept a running
   vocabulary log across modules 1–9, pick 50 entries at random, cover the English side, and
   score recall of meaning and pronunciation as a percentage — this estimates the reliability
   of your logged total. (b) Free recall: in 5 timed minutes, write every Spanish word you
   know, organised loosely by travel / daily life / social categories; count unique correct
   words. Free recall will land well under 800–1000 (timed free recall always undercounts
   real active vocabulary) — treat it as a floor, and treat the logged-total-times-recall-rate
   from (a) as the better estimate of whether you are near the 800–1000 target. Also check:
   does the vocabulary include vosotros forms and Peninsular-register items (vale, coger,
   ordenador-type usage) rather than only Latin American equivalents?
**Rubric (whole-path, three bands).**
- *Not yet*: part 1 conversation breaks down before 2–3 minutes or skips a required topic;
  part 3 shows grammar categories with more errors than correct uses; part 4 gets fewer than
  4 of 6 reading questions right; part 5's logged-vocabulary estimate falls well under 800
  words or shows little Peninsular-specific vocabulary.
- *Good enough to continue* — i.e., ready for travel use at the honest strong-A2/B1-threshold
  level this path targets: part 1 sustains 3+ minutes covering the required topics with
  occasional grammar slips you can catch on review; part 3 shows each grammar category
  correct more often than not; part 4 gets 4–5 of 6 right; part 5's estimate is in or close
  to the 800–1000 range with clear Peninsular vocabulary and vosotros forms present.
- *Solid* — beyond what 130 hours of self-study should be expected to produce, and worth
  noting rather than expecting: part 1 sustains 4–5 minutes fluidly with self-corrections
  audible; part 3 is correct throughout; part 4 is 6/6; part 5's estimate comfortably exceeds
  1000 words.
**Go/no-go.** *Good enough to continue* or *solid* on all five parts: the path has met its
retargeted goal — the learner is ready to use this Spanish on a trip, with the explicit
caveat in Open Questions below about what self-assessment could not verify. *Not yet* on any
one part: that part names which target outcome is short — return to the modules feeding that
outcome (see the outcome-coverage table in `curriculum.md`) and redo their checkpoints before
re-attempting only that part of the final check, not the whole thing. There is no fourth
attempt built into the path itself; if a gap persists after one redo cycle, it should be
treated as a real limit of the 130-hour budget against the original B1 label, consistent with
the feasibility trade-off already recorded in `requirements.md`, not a personal failure.

## Open Questions
- **Distinción and rr are close to impossible to self-detect.** A learner who pronounces
  these sounds incorrectly usually perceives themselves as correct — that is what makes them
  hard sounds in the first place. Shadow-and-compare helps but does not fully solve this: it
  catches gross errors (rr as single r) far better than subtle ones (distinción drifting
  toward seseo under fatigue). What would close this gap: a few sessions with a native speaker
  or tutor (even a single paid session, or a language-exchange partner), or a phonetic/ASR
  pronunciation-scoring tool if one becomes available within budget — neither is assumed
  available here given the €50 budget and no confirmed conversation partner.
- **Real-time, unpredictable listening comprehension cannot be fully self-assessed.** Every
  listening check in this path uses a fixed clip with a knowable answer (comprehension
  questions, then check the transcript). That tests comprehension of prepared native speech,
  not the ability to track an actual conversation partner who interrupts, rephrases, or
  responds unpredictably — which is closer to outcome 2's real target. What would close this
  gap: any live exchange with a native or fluent speaker, in person or via a language-exchange
  app/call.
- **Interactive conversational fluency (turn-taking, real-time repair) is only approximated
  by self-interview role-play.** Playing both sides of a conversation removes the central
  difficulty of actual conversation — not knowing what the other person will say next. The
  module 9 checkpoint and final check test production and self-monitoring, which are
  necessary but not sufficient for outcome 1 as stated. What would close this gap: the same
  as above — a real conversation partner, ideally several, in the weeks before the trip.
- **Free-recall vocabulary counts systematically undercount true active vocabulary** (this is
  noted inline in the final check), which is why the final check treats it as a floor and
  relies on the logged-vocabulary self-audit as the primary estimate. If the learner has not
  kept a running vocabulary log, the final check's part 5 has no reliable method at all beyond
  free recall — worth flagging early (module 1) rather than discovering at the end.
